# Bridges and trusses that must report member forces

Luban's Bridges first chained nodes with `addJoint({ kind: 'distance' })`. A 12 m truss with a 10 kN load sagged 1.89 m at a 1/60 step, kept swinging, and stretched unsymmetrically under a symmetric load (the joints are solved one after another, in one order). A joint also gives only `distance()`, not the force it carried, so a member cannot tear or crush by its load.

What works today: solve the frame yourself with small-step position constraints, and keep the engine world for the bodies that collide.

```js
// One small step h of a member between nodes a and b (inverse masses wa, wb). compliance = length / (E·A).
const C = dist - rest, alpha = compliance / (h * h);
const dl = -C / (wa + wb + alpha);   // move a by -dl·wa·n and b by +dl·wb·n
member.force = -dl / (h * h);        // newtons: pull positive, push negative
```

Rules:

- Twelve steps of 1/720 s a frame with one round each, alternating the order every step, gave symmetric forces that match a hand-worked truss.
- Smooth the force over about 0.04 s before comparing with the strength, or one jolt of a single step breaks a member.
- Let the frame take its own weight over the first moments (ramp gravity), or it drops and breaks at once.
- A rope member pulls only: skip it when it is shorter than its length.
- Carts and debris stay in the engine world: wheels are circles held together by a `distance` joint, a beast and its cart by a `rope` joint; each deck member is a kinematic box set to follow its two nodes each step, and a wheel's weight goes onto those nodes.
- A body's `angle` is in degrees (see body-angle-degrees).
